\subsubsection{Split-substrate composition: a learned K/V prefix and an in-context block}
\label{sec:method_split}

We test whether a host can read one memory from two substrates at once. The 32 queried channels
(ch8--39) are partitioned into disjoint sets $C_A$ and $C_B$ by interleaving ($C_A$ takes even channels
first; $|C_A| \in \{4, 8, 16\}$). \textbf{Block~A is delivered only through a learned prefix}: a write head
maps the $|C_A|$ channel values to a synthetic K/V cache occupying positions $[0, P{-}1]$ of the host's
attention layers, with $P = 513$ (160 for GPT-2 Medium, whose 1{,}024 absolute positions cannot hold a
513-position prefix beside a 512-token block and an answer). \textbf{Block~B is delivered only through the
tokens}: the FDM signal placed at position $P$ encodes $C_B$ together with the never-queried channels
0--7, and the carriers for $C_A$ are omitted from the signal entirely. A leak check confirms the design on
every cell: two memories that differ only in Block~A produce identical tokens (30/30 trials), and changing
one Block~B value changes them (30/30). Block~A is therefore recoverable from the prefix or not at all.

Only the write head is trained (AdamW, cosine schedule, 5{,}000 steps for $|C_A|{=}4$ and 15{,}000 for
$|C_A| \ge 8$, cross-entropy on the answer tokens); the host is frozen throughout. For Qwen3-0.6B and
Hermes3-3B the host is the two-block checkpoint of the previous paragraph. For LFM2.5-1.2B and GPT-2
Medium the host is the single-block Stage-4 checkpoint of \S2.1.3 with no two-block finetuning at all. The
write head emits K/V for every attention layer the host has: all 28 for Qwen3 and Hermes3 (62.7M
parameters), all 24 for GPT-2 (54.2M), and only the 6 attention layers of the hybrid LFM2.5 (10.3M); its
10 short-convolution layers never see the prefix. Two controls accompany each cell. The
\emph{shuffled-prefix control} evaluates the trained head with a prefix built from a different random memory:
Block~A must fall to chance (about 26\%) while Block~B holds, and we report $\Delta_A = $ matched minus
control. The \emph{masked baseline} runs the host alone, with no prefix, on a block from which the $|C_A|$
carriers have been removed; it measures the distribution shift that per-sample renormalisation of the
remaining carriers imposes on Block~B. Scoring takes the first \texttt{NAME=VALUE} occurrence per channel.
Each cell is three seeds with $n{=}200$ held-out memories per seed.

\subsection{Reading one memory from two substrates}
\label{sec:results_split}

Table~\ref{tab:split_substrate} gives slot accuracy for Block~A (prefix only) and Block~B (context only)
on all four hosts. \textbf{At 4/28 and 8/24 the two substrates behave as designed on every host.} Block~A is
read from the prefix at 99.9--100\% and collapses to chance (24--27\%) when the prefix is shuffled, a drop of
72--76 points against a full-collapse reference of about 73; Block~B is unchanged by the shuffle, moving by
at most 0.3 points. Since the leak check proves Block~A is absent from the tokens, the K/V prefix is its
only route, and the host reads both regions in one forward pass.

\textbf{The mechanism does not depend on two-block finetuning or on a full-depth prefix.} LFM2.5-1.2B
and GPT-2 Medium are single-block hosts that were never trained on a second region, yet both reach 100\%
on Block~A at 4/28 and 8/24. On LFM2.5 only 6 of 16 layers receive the prefix and the write head is six
times smaller than on the transformers; on GPT-2 the prefix is 160 positions rather than 513. Neither
matters at these splits.

\textbf{16/16 is the capacity edge, and hosts fail differently.} Hermes3-3B still reads 92.0\% of Block~A;
LFM2.5 reaches 84.6\%, GPT-2 78.7\%, and Qwen3-0.6B 50.0\%. On Qwen3 each seed learns three or four
channels to about 100\% and leaves the rest at chance, with a different subset per seed, so the limit is
head optimisation rather than a property of any channel. Prefix length is not the limit: GPT-2 does better
than Qwen3 with a prefix less than a third as long. Block~B stays at or near ceiling on Qwen3 and Hermes3
throughout. On the two single-block hosts, Block~B degrades with the number of carriers removed from the
signal (masked baseline 99.8 $\to$ 76.8 on LFM2.5, 94.8 $\to$ 66.2 on GPT-2) and a trained prefix,
matched or shuffled, partly restores it (to 94.4 and 91.9 at 16/16): the head learns to compensate the
renormalisation shift as well as to carry Block~A. For LFM2.5 at 16/16 this required the host's own
assessment sentence in the write-head loss; with it masked out, all three heads corrupted the preamble and
one pushed Block~B below the no-prefix baseline (75.0 / 77.6 at 16/16; Appendix~X).

The earlier sweep reported in a previous version of this paper, in which the token block still encoded every
channel, measured tolerance to a learned prefix rather than retrieval from it. The present design removes
that ambiguity.
